# 5. AI Content Principles

## AI Should Not Always Be the Villain

AI can be:

- Helpful
- Overconfident
- Surprisingly useful
- Confused
- Technically correct but practically wrong
- Doing exactly what it was asked
- Making developers' lives easier
- Making developers' lives strangely more complicated

This creates more nuanced humor than:

> **"AI bad."**

---

## Prefer Visual Humor

The image should ideally communicate the joke before the reader reaches
the caption.

Strong patterns:

> **Confident AI + chaotic environment**

> **Simple request + absurd implementation**

> **Technically correct answer + practically wrong outcome**

---

## Keep Image Text Minimal

Prefer:

**One main sentence**

-

**One tiny punchline**

over large paragraphs.

The image should be understood visually first.

# 7. AI Content Positioning

The AI category should communicate:

> **AI is incredibly useful. That's exactly why we should understand its
> limitations.**

The humor should make people laugh first and think second.

The strongest posts should leave the reader thinking:

> **"Yep. I've seen that."**

or:

> **"Okay, that's painfully accurate."**

The category should remain:

**Funny → Relatable → Developer-specific → Occasionally thoughtful**

rather than:

**AI news → AI hype → AI fear → AI promotion**

---

# 8. Relationship With Medium

AI humor posts should generally remain independent from Medium
promotion.

Use funny AI posts to build:

**Recognition → Engagement → Trust**

Then occasionally publish a deeper Medium article when there is a
genuinely useful technical idea behind the joke.

Possible flow:

```text
Funny AI Post
      ↓
Developer relates
      ↓
Technical observation
      ↓
Deeper explanation
      ↓
Medium article
```

The Medium link should feel like an extension of the conversation, not
an advertisement.

# 9. Visual Identity

Maintain the established visual language:

- Minimalist hand-drawn developer doodle
- White or slightly warm-white paper
- Dark graphite / black ink
- Imperfect organic lines
- Handwritten annotations
- Simple technical diagrams
- Generous whitespace
- Human developer-notebook feeling

Use restrained accent colors:

- Muted green for positive/successful elements
- Muted red for errors/warnings
- Never overload the illustration with color

Avoid:

- Photorealism
- 3D
- Glossy interfaces
- Corporate stock-art
- Cyberpunk/futuristic styling
- Polished vector infographic aesthetics
- Dense text
- Overly complicated illustrations

---

# 10. Recommended AI Workflow

For every new AI idea:

1.  Pick an idea from the backlog.
2.  Decide whether it should be pure humor or humor + engineering
    insight.
3.  Identify the visual joke.
4.  Keep the image concept simple.
5.  Write the image-generation prompt.
6.  Generate the doodle.
7.  Refine the image if necessary.
8.  Write the short LinkedIn caption.
9.  Add a technical takeaway when appropriate.
10. Publish.
11. Track which style of AI humor resonates most.

The goal is not to post every AI idea.

The goal is to find a recognizable style of:

> **Developer humor about the strange reality of working with AI.**
